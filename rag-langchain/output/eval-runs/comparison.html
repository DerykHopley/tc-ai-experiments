Comparing retrieval strategies

The same 13 test questions, run through the pipeline with different ways of picking the top 6 chunks (or none), and scored by the same judge (google/gemini-2.5-flash).

  • top-k: the 6 nearest chunks. One artist can fill several slots.
  • two-per-artist: the nearest chunks, at most 2 from any artist.
  • one-per-artist: the nearest chunk from each of 6 different artists.
  • rerank (the ■ squares): the 30 nearest chunks, scored 0–3 for usefulness by an LLM (gpt-4o-mini); the best 6 are kept.
  • long-context (the ◆ diamonds): no search at all. Every artist’s whole profile goes into the prompt, about 28k tokens. The baseline that shows what retrieval adds; its retrieval isn’t scored.

Reading this

  • Compare against the noise band first. Two runs with identical settings already differ, because answers and the judge’s verdicts vary.
  • Retrieval scores don’t use an LLM, so they only change when the retrieved chunks change. Generation scores can change on any run.
  • Each run’s full details (every claim and verdict) are on its own page: top-k-a, top-k-b, two-per-artist, one-per-artist, rerank-a, rerank-b, rerank-c, long-context-a, long-context-b.
